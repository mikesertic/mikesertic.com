Speaking

I speak about self-government as a practice: how people figure out what they actually believe, and how organizations run without a chain of command. Audiences have ranged from student conferences to party conventions to teams exploring self-management.

Talks

Each of these can be a keynote, a workshop, or a conversation. Titles and length can be adjusted to your program.

Politically Homeless: What 30 Million Quiz Takers Reveal About the Two-Party Map

The World's Smallest Political Quiz has been taken by approximately 30 million people since 1985, and a large share of them do not land where the two-party map says they should. This talk walks through what the Quiz measures, why the left-to-right line hides more than it shows, and what changes in civic engagement once people can see where they actually stand.

For general, political, and student audiences.

Self-Government as a Daily Practice: Lessons From Morning Star

Morning Star processes a large share of America's tomatoes with thousands of colleagues and no managers. Each person writes agreements with the people they work with and is accountable to them directly. Drawing on time at the company's Self-Management Institute, this talk covers how the system works in practice, where it strains, and which of its habits any team can adopt this quarter.

For business, nonprofit, and leadership audiences.

Liberty You Can Run: Why the Freedom Movement Should Care How Its Own Organizations Work

The liberty movement has spent decades arguing for self-government in politics while running most of its own organizations by command. This talk makes the case that a self-managed organization is the most persuasive demonstration of liberty available, and shows how liberty groups, campus chapters, and nonprofits can practice the principle inside their own walls.

For liberty movement conferences and organizations.

Teaching Politics Without Taking Sides: Nonpartisan Civic Education That Works

Quiz in the Classroom brings the World's Smallest Political Quiz to students without telling them what to think. This talk explains how the program is designed, what teachers report, and why self-assessment holds attention in classrooms where advocacy loses it.

For educators, school leaders, and education policy audiences.

Your Political DNA: 32 Archetypes and the End of the Single Axis

Political identity has more dimensions than one line can hold. This talk introduces the Political DNA framework, the 32 archetypes it produces, and what the results suggest about coalitions, persuasion, and the voters that pollsters keep miscounting.

For media, political, and general audiences.

Running a Nonprofit Like a Self-Governing Organization

Since 2017 I have led a nonprofit founded in 1985 through a rebuild of its systems and programs. This practical talk covers what I applied from Morning Star, including clear written agreements, distributed authority, and measuring what matters, and what I would do differently.

For nonprofit leaders, boards, and founders.
